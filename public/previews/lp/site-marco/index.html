(03) Investor Guide

How to Buy Property in Brazil

Brazil imposes no restrictions on foreign ownership. The process is straightforward when guided correctly — here's what to expect.

Marco coordinates the entire process. From CPF guidance to lawyer introduction, BACEN registration and Cartório filing — so you never navigate Brazilian bureaucracy alone. International wire transfers are fully legal when correctly declared to BACEN, ensuring the right to repatriate funds in the future.

01

Get Your CPF

The CPF (Cadastro de Pessoas Físicas) is Brazil's taxpayer ID. Foreign nationals can obtain it remotely via a Brazilian consulate or in-country in one visit. Required for all property transactions.

Pre-Purchase · 1–2 weeks
02

Property Selection

Marco presents exclusive listings matched to your criteria and budget. Each property is pre-vetted for title clarity, debt status, and zoning compliance before being shown to buyers.

Marco's Exclusive List
03

Due Diligence

A certified Brazilian lawyer conducts a full title search (matrícula), checks for liens, taxes owed, and legal disputes. Standard turnaround: 5–10 business days. Cost typically 0.5–1% of purchase price.

Legal · Recommended
04

Purchase Agreement

A "Promessa de Compra e Venda" (purchase and sale agreement) is signed. A deposit of 10–30% is typically paid at this stage. The agreement is legally binding and registered at a Cartório.

Contract · Notarised
05

ITBI & Transfer Deed

The ITBI (municipal property transfer tax) applies at 2–4% of the declared purchase value, depending on the municipality. The "Escritura Pública de Compra e Venda" is then signed before a notary.

Tax · 2–4% ITBI
06

Registration & Title

The notarised deed is registered at the local Cartório de Registro de Imóveis. Once registered, you are the legal owner. Currency transfer is declared to Brazil's Central Bank (BACEN) for repatriation rights.

Title · Freehold
